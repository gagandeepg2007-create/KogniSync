## Before the day

### One week out
- [ ] Every member can build and run the project from a clean checkout
- [ ] Accounts, API keys and quotas obtained and **tested** — not just requested
- [ ] The provided dataset loaded and queried by at least two people on the team
- [ ] `tools/validate_conformance.py` run once, and it passes
- [ ] You have decided who does what, and who owns the demo

### The day before
- [ ] Repository created, everyone has push access, everyone has cloned it
- [ ] Dependencies installed on every machine — do not plan to install over venue wi-fi
- [ ] A hello-world deploy of your stack running end to end
- [ ] Laptops, chargers, adapters, a power strip and a spare mouse
- [ ] Sleep. Genuinely. The teams that demo badly are usually the ones that did not.

### How to spend the first hour

Not writing code. Spend it agreeing three things: the single flow you will demo, who owns which piece, and what you will cut first if you fall behind. Teams that skip this hour lose four later.

### Mentors

Mentors are there to unblock, review an approach and ask the awkward question. They will not write your code or debug your hardware. Come with a specific question — "our retrieval returns the wrong city, here is the query" gets a useful answer; "can you look at our project" does not.

### The 09:00 rule

At 09:00 on the second morning, stop adding features. Every hackathon has at least one team that had a working demo at 09:00, added one more thing, and had nothing at 12:00. The last three hours are for stabilising and rehearsing.

### What a good presentation does

1. **Show the thing running.** In the first two minutes. A working build beats a polished deck, and nothing loses a room faster than four slides before the demo.
2. **Demo the one flow you rehearsed**, end to end, without apologising for what is missing.
3. **Show the AI feature actually working** — live, on real data, not a screenshot.
4. **Say what you cut and why.** Deliberate scope reads as judgement.
5. **Have the fallback ready.** A recorded run of your demo, on the machine, offline. Venue wi-fi has ended more demos than bad code.

### Practicalities

- Test your display adapter before your slot, not during it
- Have the app already running and warmed up before you are called
- Close everything else on the machine
- Decide who speaks. Two voices maximum

## Rules

- Teams of 3–5. One problem statement per team.
- Building happens inside the 24 hours. Preparation — reading, learning the dataset, environment setup, a scaffold — is expected and encouraged before the sprint.
- Use any libraries, frameworks and AI tools you like. Say what you used.
- The provided dataset's mandatory core fields must not be renamed or re-keyed. Adding your own tables and columns beside them is encouraged.
- Everything must fit the Travel & Tourism theme and read as part of a coherent travel portal.
- Be decent to each other, to the mentors and to the venue.
